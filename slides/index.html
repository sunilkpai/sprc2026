The photonic mesh and its applications

Each box is a Mach–Zehnder interferometer: phase φ, a 50:50 coupler, phase θ, a second coupler. A rectangular mesh of them implements any N×N unitary.

Applications
  • Universal linear optics
    Reck 1994, Clements 2016
  • Self-configuring optics
    Miller 2013: mode sorting, unscrambling light, free-space links
  • Quantum photonics
    Carolan 2015: reconfigurable interferometers for photons
  • Neural networks
    Shen 2017: matrix multiplies for deep learning
  • Programmable photonics
    Bogaerts 2020: an FPGA for light
The mesh is one device with five literatures. Everything in this talk is about the fourth bullet and what it borrowed from the other four.

The 2010s thesis: photonic compute for quantum and AI

Quantum: the mesh is the computer
  • Linear optics, single photons, and detectors are universal (KLM 2001)
  • Reprogrammable 6-mode universal interferometer on a chip (Carolan et al., Science 2015)
  • Boson sampling, then fusion- and measurement-based architectures built from switches and interferometers
AI: the mesh as a matrix engine
  • Shen et al., Nat. Photonics 2017: coherent nanophotonic deep learning
  • Premise: N² multiply-accumulates per pass for O(N) conversions and no data movement
  • Training energy doubling every few months; a digital MAC costs ≈ 100 fJ

Well over $1B of venture funding followed the AI thesis between 2017 and 2023.

Two communities adopted the same hardware for opposite reasons. Quantum needs the mesh to be exact. AI needs it to be cheap. Keep that tension in mind.

The post-GPT thesis: photonic interconnects

Where the bottleneck went
  • Transformer decode is memory-bandwidth bound: GPUs wait on HBM and on each other
  • Models grew 100×+, clusters 10×, electrical I/O about 2×
  • Co-packaged optics replaces pluggables: Nvidia photonic switches (2025), TSMC COUPE, Broadcom
  • The AI datacenter adopted photonics for moving numbers, not multiplying them

Optical neural network theory

A layer is a mesh plus a nonlinearity
\[ \mathbf{x}^{(\ell+1)} = f\!\left(M^{(\ell)}\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)}\right),\qquad M = V\,\Sigma\,U^{\dagger} \]

Any weight matrix is two unitaries and a diagonal of attenuators, so two meshes per layer suffice. One MZI is a 2×2 unitary \(T(\theta,\phi)\); \(N(N-1)/2\) of them in a grid reach all of \(\mathrm{U}(N)\) (Reck 1994; Clements 2016).

Where the energy goes, per multiply-accumulate
\[ E_{\mathrm{MAC}} \approx \underbrace{\frac{E_{\mathrm{DAC}}+E_{\mathrm{ADC}}}{N}}_{\text{conversions}} + \underbrace{\frac{2^{2b}\,\hbar\omega}{\eta\,N}}_{\text{photons, } b \text{ bits}} + \underbrace{\frac{P_{\mathrm{hold}}}{f_{\mathrm{clk}}\,N}}_{\text{holding phases}} \]
What the scaling says
  • Every term falls as 1/N: the mesh pays off only when it is wide
  • Photons are cheap: 10⁵ photons per 8-bit detection is well under a femtojoule per MAC
  • Conversions are not: a GS/s 8-bit ADC costs picojoules, so N must reach the hundreds
  • Footprint caps N near 64 to 256 per reticle, and error and loss compound over N stages
The bet
Optics wins if fan-in N is large, precision b is low, weights change slowly, and the data is already analog. In 2017, none of those described an AI workload.
The equation is the whole talk. Three terms, all over N. Hold that thought for the "what changed" slide, because two of the four conditions in the box flipped after 2022.

Why width is hard: light through a 128-mesh

  • A random unitary is not a random set of phases: Haar-distributed meshes concentrate the MZIs near cross state toward the centre (top row)
  • Initialise uniformly and light collapses onto a few paths (bottom row); the gradient landscape has the same structure
  • Every error term in the energy equation compounds over the 2N stages light crosses, so width buys efficiency and costs accuracy at the same rate
  • This is the mesh-specific problem the next two slides solve in hardware: measure the gradient of the device you have, not the one you designed
Optional slide; drop it if short on time. The point is that the mesh parametrisation is far from the matrix it represents, which is why training it needs the device in the loop.

In situ backpropagation and inverse design

Same trick as photonic inverse design
  • Adjoint method: a gradient is the interference of a forward and a backward field (Hughes, Minkov, Fan 2018)
  • Forward input, backward error, then their sum: tap power at each phase shifter reads out \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\theta\)
What the chip did
  • 3-layer, 4-port silicon photonic network with thermal phase shifters and grating taps imaged by an IR camera
  • Analog update: one toggle applies the gradient, with no digital subtraction and no O(N²) readback
  • Ring and moons trained on chip: 90 to 98% test accuracy, tracking digital training curves
  • Simulated 64×64 MNIST: 97% ideal, 95% with tap noise and I/O error
Point at the three colored paths. Forward, backward, sum. The gradient is a power measurement. This is the same adjoint I used at Google X for inverse design; the chip is just a differentiable physical system.

Inference versus training: the energy budget

Digital bars: datasheet peak dense throughput over board power. Envise: 65.5 TOPS at 78 W electrical plus 1.6 W optical (Harris, Apr 2025). Formats differ; read as orders of magnitude.

Reading the chart: a 2025 mesh system sits about 10× above GPU peak, with 98% of its power in electronics. The optics are not the term to optimize.

Inference: one forward pass
  • Tolerates 4 to 8 bits, the analog-friendly regime
  • Weights static, so phase holding amortizes
  • Cost is dominated by conversions, not photons
Training: forward, backward, update
  • Gradients want 16-bit dynamic range, analog's weak spot
  • In situ backprop: the backward pass is one more optical pass, and the update is O(1) per phase instead of O(N²) memory traffic
  • But each step rewrites N² phases, so weight write energy and speed set the budget
Be careful with the chart: peak datasheet numbers versus a measured research system. The point is not the ratio, it is which term dominates. In situ training removes the memory traffic term, but it does not touch the conversion term.

Where the energy goes: the 2023 model, Envise, and a 4-bit projection

2023 SM model at N = 128, 1 GHz, segmented phase shifters for inputs and weights (no DACs); Envise from the Nature 2025 SI; projection rules in the notes.

Reading the chart: 4 bits is an inference story: the ADC and the light collapse and inference sits 7× under the 4-bit digital line. Training needs precision: at 8-bit readout it is 3 to 5× under the 8-bit digital line, and pushing the taps to 12 bits costs 256× the light and lands above FP16 digital.

Chart basis: Science 2023 SM Tables S1 to S4 at N equals 128 and 1 gigahertz. Envise: 78 watts plus 1.6 watts optical at 65.5 TOPS, 0.25 watts in the encode path including its weight DACs, from the Nature 2025 SI. Assumptions behind the 4-bit projection: ADC energy scales as 2 to the bits (Walden); the Nature SI's thermal-limited 4 to the bits would be 16 times more optimistic. Digital op divided by 3 is the B200 FP4 to H100 INT8 ratio at the wall. Optical power scales as 4 to the bits from shot noise, so 4-bit inference needs 256 times less light and 12-bit gradient readout needs 256 times more; that is the 3.4 picojoule per op bar, 3 picojoules of it laser. Training is not projected to 4 bits because gradients need precision. Segmented phase shifters everywhere means every weight cell needs b digital lines from the control die: 65 thousand contacts at 4 bits for a 128 mesh. Three things to say. One: the 2023 tables already predicted that conversions would dominate, and Envise confirmed it with a factor of 40 between the tensor core and the package. Two: 4-bit inference is where the optics win on paper. Three: training does not get the same gift, because the tap photodiodes need enough light to see a shrinking gradient, and the TIA per phase shifter is bit-independent.

Rack design when the accelerator is 100 W, not 1 kW

Where the heat is, per 128-wide mesh
  • Light, ADCs, TIAs, laser: ≈2 W
  • Thermal phase shifters: 16,384 × 50 mW ≈ 800 W. Segments must be EO or MEMS
  • Digital shell: Envise measured 19 W per core, 25× the optics. This sets the package power; HBM adds 25 to 30 W per stack
Cooling changes purpose
  • Capacity: gone. A 50 to 100 W package is air-cooled
  • Stability: stays. Si dn/dT is 0.07 rad/K per 100 µm, so sub-kelvin uniformity: TEC per die or a low-flow loop
  • Lasers: one water-cooled shelf per rack, fibre to compute and CPO ports. The only liquid in the rack

What it means
  • Same throughput at a fifth of the power in any air-cooled hall, no CDU or manifolds; or 1,200 accelerators in the same 120 kW, a network problem before a thermal one
  • The rack was dense because copper NVLink reaches 1 to 2 m. Photonic I/O removes the reason; photonic compute removes its cost
This slide is the discussion point. The claim is not that photonics is cold, it is that the kilowatt package and the metre of copper were both choices. Say the thermal-phase-shifter number out loud: 800 watts per mesh is what happens if you keep heaters. Then the honest part: the package power is the digital shell, and Envise measured 25 times the photonic core. If the shell gets to 50 femtojoules per op, an NVL72's worth of ops fits in an air-cooled rack. Lasers are the one thing that still wants water, so pool them. Also in the doc but not on the slide: PUE gives a quarter back, air 1.3 to 1.5 against liquid 1.1, and memory and network power do not change.

What changed after 2022?

Precision collapsed
FP16 in 2017, FP8 with Hopper in 2022, FP4 with Blackwell in 2024. Inference at 4 bits is routine. The industry walked into the regime analog optics always needed.
Attention moved the bottleneck
Decode is KV-cache and weight-bandwidth bound. Softmax, normalization, and memory traffic are not matrix multiplies. A faster MVM engine attacks the wrong term.
Inference became the product
ChatGPT (Nov 2022), then reasoning models with test-time compute (2024). Inference now dominates deployed FLOPs and spend. Training chips are a market of a few buyers.
The ADC wall stayed put
Conversion energy per sample improves far slower than logic (Murmann survey). Every photonic accelerator still pays it on every activation, in and out.

Revisit the four conditions: fan-in large (still hard), precision low (now true), weights static (true for inference), data already analog (not in a datacenter).

Two of the four flipped in our favor. The two that did not are both about where the data lives, which is the hint for the last slide.

Other applications: quantum computing and analog signal processing

Quantum photonics: exactness first
  • Meshes are the interferometers, switches, and multiplexers of linear-optical quantum computers (Carolan 2015; Xanadu and silicon platforms, Nature 2025)
  • No DAC or ADC in the data path: photons in, detector clicks out
  • Loss, extinction, and phase accuracy are what matter; accelerator calibration methods transfer directly
Analog signal processing and sensing
  • Mode sorting and unscrambling for free-space and multimode links (Miller 2013; Annoni 2017)
  • RF photonic filtering and beamforming, lidar wavefront control, optical switching
  • The signal is already light: conversions only at the boundary, weights updated at kHz by feedback
The transferable toolkit: parallel programming of feedforward meshes, power monitoring with two detectors instead of taps, and in situ gradient measurement. Each was built to make an accelerator trustworthy; each is worth more where the mesh is the signal path. Pai et al., JSTQE 2020; Nanophotonics 2023; Science 2023.
Do not land a conclusion here. Just show that the same three papers have an obvious home in two fields that never needed the GPU comparison.
