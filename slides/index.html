The photonic mesh and its applications

Each box is a Mach–Zehnder interferometer: phase \(\phi\), a 50:50 coupler, phase \(\theta\), a second coupler. Sweep \(\theta\) and \(\phi\) and the light can be steered entirely into one port, which is how a mesh is programmed and how it is read out. A rectangular mesh of them implements any \(N\times N\) unitary.

Applications
  • Universal linear optics
    Reck 1994, Clements 2016
  • Self-configuring optics
    Miller 2013: mode sorting, unscrambling light, free-space links
  • Quantum photonics
    Carolan 2015: reconfigurable interferometers for photons
  • Neural networks
    Shen 2017: matrix multiplies for deep learning
  • Programmable photonics
    Bogaerts 2020: an FPGA for light
[0:20] The mesh is one device with five literatures. Everything in this talk is about the fourth bullet and what it borrowed from the other four.

The 2010s thesis: photonic compute for quantum and AI

Quantum: the mesh is the computer
  • Linear optics, single photons, and detectors are universal (KLM 2001)
  • Reprogrammable 6-mode universal interferometer on a chip (Carolan et al., Science 2015)
  • Boson sampling, then fusion- and measurement-based architectures built from switches and interferometers
AI: the mesh as a matrix engine
  • Shen et al., Nat. Photonics 2017: coherent nanophotonic deep learning
  • Premise: \(N^2\) multiply-accumulates per pass for \(O(N)\) conversions and no data movement
  • Training energy doubling every few months; a digital MAC costs \(\approx100\,\mathrm{fJ}\)

Well over $1B of venture funding followed the AI thesis between 2017 and 2023.

[1:00] Two communities adopted the same hardware for opposite reasons. Quantum needs the mesh to be exact. AI needs it to be cheap. Keep that tension in mind.

The bottleneck moved to interconnect

Optical neural network theory

A layer is a mesh plus a nonlinearity
\[ \mathbf{x}^{(\ell+1)} = f\!\left(M^{(\ell)}\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)}\right),\qquad M = V\,\Sigma\,U^{\dagger} \]

Any weight matrix is two unitaries and a diagonal of attenuators, so two meshes per layer suffice. One MZI is a \(2\times2\) unitary \(T(\theta,\phi)\); \(N(N-1)/2\) of them in a grid reach all of \(\mathrm{U}(N)\) (Reck 1994; Clements 2016).

Energy per multiply-accumulate
\[ E_{\mathrm{MAC}} \approx \underbrace{\frac{E_{\mathrm{DAC}}+E_{\mathrm{ADC}}}{N}}_{\text{conversions}} + \underbrace{\frac{2^{2b}\,\hbar\omega}{\eta\,N}}_{\text{photons, } b \text{ bits}} + \underbrace{\frac{P_{\mathrm{hold}}}{f_{\mathrm{clk}}\,N}}_{\text{holding phases}} \]
Consequences of the \(1/N\) scaling
  • Every term falls as \(1/N\): the mesh pays off only when it is wide
  • Photons are cheap: \(10^5\) photons per 8-bit detection is well under \(1\,\mathrm{fJ}\) per MAC
  • Conversions are not: a \(\mathrm{GS/s}\) 8-bit ADC costs picojoules, so \(N\) must reach the hundreds
  • Footprint caps \(N\) near \(64\) to \(256\) per reticle, and error and loss compound over \(N\) stages
The bet
Optics wins if fan-in \(N\) is large, precision \(b\) is low, weights change slowly, and the data is already analog. In 2017, none of those described an AI workload.
[2:30] The equation is the whole talk. Three terms, all over N. Hold that thought for the "what changed" slide, because two of the four conditions in the box flipped after 2022.

In situ backpropagation: the method

1 Forward. Send the input \(x\) through the mesh, read the output, and record the tap power \(p_\eta\) at every phase shifter.
2 Backward. Send the error vector \(y_{\mathrm{adj}}\) in from the output side. Reciprocity gives \(U^{T}\) for free; record \(p_{\eta,\mathrm{adj}}\).
3 Sum. Send \(x - i\,x_{\mathrm{adj}}^{*}\) forward. The tap power now contains the interference term, and the gradient of every phase is read out at once.
\[ \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\eta} = -\,\mathrm{Im}\!\left(x_\eta\,x_{\mathrm{adj},\eta}\right) = \tfrac12\left(p_{\eta,\mathrm{sum}} - p_\eta - p_{\eta,\mathrm{adj}}\right) = \tfrac14\left(p_{\eta,+} - p_{\eta,-}\right) \]

Three optical passes give the gradient of all \(N(N-1)\) phases of the device you actually have, with no model of its imperfections. Same adjoint as photonic inverse design (Hughes, Minkov, Fan 2018).

Fig. 1E of Pai et al., Science 380, 398 (2023). Digital subtraction uses three passes; the analog update toggles the adjoint phase and reads the AC term directly.
[3:30] Point at the three colored paths. Forward, backward, sum. The gradient is a power measurement: interference between the forward field and the back-propagated error at each phase shifter, exactly the adjoint method from inverse design, and it does not care about fabrication error because the chip is doing the differentiation. Third formula is the analog version: toggle the adjoint phase, high-pass filter, done.

The experiment: a \(6\times6\) mesh, a camera, and an analog update

  • A, B: 15 MZIs, TiN heaters, a \(3\%\) grating tap beside every shifter, \(6\times19\) spots imaged by an IR camera on an XY stage; fiber arrays on both sides for forward and backward light
  • C to F: the analog update. Toggle the adjoint phase \(\zeta\) between \(0\) and \(\pi\); the AC component of the tap power is the gradient. Integrate it and write the voltage. No subtraction, no \(O(N^2)\) readback, error on par with the digital route and batchable by linearity
Fig. 2 and Fig. S1 of Pai et al. (2023). Setup: microscope on a movable stage (blue), fiber arrays (red), fiber switch (orange), IR camera (purple). Ring and moons trained on chip to \(90\) to \(98\%\); \(1000\) iterations took \(31\) hours of stage motion.
[4:30] This is what a research demonstration looks like: the detector is a camera, the "ADC" is a microscope stage, and the phase shifters are heaters at 50 milliwatts each. Panels C to F are the part to dwell on: the analog update. Toggle the adjoint phase, the AC part of the tap power is the gradient, integrate it over the batch, write the voltage. That is the batch-gradient story on the energy slide, demonstrated on one MZI with op-amps. Every number in the energy model later replaces one of these with an integrated part. The point of the slide is that the protocol worked on a chip with real loss and real crosstalk.

Energy per operation: the 2023 model, Envise, and a 4-bit projection

2023 SM model at \(N=128\), \(1\,\mathrm{GHz}\), segmented phase shifters for inputs and weights (no DACs); Envise from the Nature 2025 SI; projection rules in the notes.

At 4 bits the ADC and the light collapse and inference sits \(7\times\) under the 4-bit digital line. Training wins on batch integration: the gradient at each tap is integrated over the batch and read once, so the 12-bit ADC runs at \(f/M\) and averaging adds \(\sqrt{M}\) in SNR. At \(M=4096\) that is \(53\,\mathrm{fJ/op}\), 6 extra gradient bits, 8-bit light.

[6:15] Chart basis: Science 2023 SM Tables S1 to S4 at N equals 128 and 1 gigahertz. Envise: 78 watts plus 1.6 watts optical at 65.5 TOPS, 0.25 watts in the encode path including its weight DACs, from the Nature 2025 SI. Assumptions behind the 4-bit projection: ADC energy scales as 2 to the bits (Walden); the Nature SI's thermal-limited 4 to the bits would be 16 times more optimistic. Digital op divided by 3 is the B200 FP4 to H100 INT8 ratio at the wall. Optical power scales as 4 to the bits from shot noise, so 4-bit inference needs 256 times less light and 12-bit gradient readout needs 256 times more; that is the 3.4 picojoule per op bar, 3 picojoules of it laser. Training is not projected to 4 bits because gradients need precision; instead the gradient readout is batch-integrated, which is what the SM's analog update does: the tap current is integrated over the batch and digitised once per phase shifter per batch, so a slow 12 to 16 bit ADC at f over M is enough and the average gains half of log2 M bits, six at M equals 4096. Realistic M today is thousands of tokens per weight per device for an LLM micro-batch and millions of positions per layer for a CNN, so the SM's 16 to 256 was conservative. Two ADCs, two rates: activations at 8 bits and 1 gigasample, gradients at 12 plus bits and megasamples. Segmented phase shifters everywhere means every weight cell needs b digital lines from the control die: 65 thousand contacts at 4 bits for a 128 mesh. Three things to say. One: the 2023 tables already predicted that conversions would dominate, and Envise confirmed it with a factor of 40 between the tensor core and the package. Two: 4-bit inference is where the optics win on paper. Three: training gets a different gift, time: integrate the gradient over the batch and precision comes from averaging, not from light or from a fast ADC. Systematic tap errors do not average, so calibration is the price.

Rack design when the accelerator is \(100\,\mathrm{W}\), not \(1\,\mathrm{kW}\)

Heat per 128-wide mesh
  • Light, ADCs, TIAs, laser: \(\approx2\,\mathrm{W}\)
  • Thermal phase shifters: \(16{,}384\times50\,\mathrm{mW}\approx800\,\mathrm{W}\). Segments must be EO or MEMS
  • Digital shell: Envise measured \(19\,\mathrm{W}\) per core, \(25\times\) the optics. This sets the package power; HBM adds \(25\) to \(30\,\mathrm{W}\) per stack
Cooling: capacity versus stability
  • Capacity: gone. A \(50\) to \(100\,\mathrm{W}\) package is air-cooled
  • Stability: stays. Si \(dn/dT\) gives \(0.07\,\mathrm{rad/K}\) per \(100\,\mu\mathrm{m}\), so sub-kelvin uniformity: TEC per die or a low-flow loop
  • Lasers: one water-cooled shelf per rack, fibre to compute and CPO ports. The only liquid in the rack

[7:15] This slide is the discussion point. The claim is not that photonics is cold, it is that the kilowatt package and the metre of copper were both choices. Say the thermal-phase-shifter number out loud: 800 watts per mesh is what happens if you keep heaters. Then the honest part: the package power is the digital shell, and Envise measured 25 times the photonic core. If the shell gets to 50 femtojoules per op, an NVL72's worth of ops fits in an air-cooled rack. Lasers are the one thing that still wants water, so pool them. Also in the doc but not on the slide: PUE gives a quarter back, air 1.3 to 1.5 against liquid 1.1, and memory and network power do not change.

  • A 128-wide mesh of 2023 MZIs is \(80\times16\,\mathrm{mm}\): bigger than a reticle. Compact cells: \(250\,\mathrm{mm}^2\) for one layer
  • Wavelength sets the floor at \(\sim10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mm}^2\) per \(2\times2\) cell; transistors keep shrinking. \(30\) to \(120\times\) less throughput per \(\mathrm{mm}^2\) than a GPU
  • The stationary weight and the footprint are the same decision: no weight traffic, but a device per parameter
Temporal mapping (Opticore; Hamerly et al., PRX 2019)
  • Weights arrive as optical pulses and multiply the input at a homodyne detector: \(O(N)\) devices, \(N\) steps, zero silicon per parameter, parameters live in HBM
  • MACs per device per step is the same as a mesh; the win is \(10\) to \(100\,\mathrm{GS/s}\) modulators and \(N\) devices instead of \(N^2\) per reticle
  • The cost: every weight is fetched and modulated on every use. HBM at \(2\) to \(4\,\mathrm{pJ/bit}\) needs batch reuse \(\gtrsim300\) to get under \(50\,\mathrm{fJ/op}\). The footprint problem becomes the memory-bandwidth problem, the one GPUs already have
Time-multiplexing the mesh
  • Block-level: EO phase shifters (\(\mathrm{ns}\)), the \(N^2 b\) contact array run at speed (\(64^2\times8\) bits per \(100\,\mathrm{ns}\), \(330\,\mathrm{Gb/s}\)), a batch per block. Keeps \(O(N)\) conversions per block and in situ backprop
  • Or a passive DFT (star coupler, \(0.1\,\mathrm{mm}^2\)) with \(N\) time-driven diagonal modulators: the smallest photonic MVM, but general matrices need \(\sim2N\) factors, so it pays only for structured layers
[7:50] This is the slide for the skeptic in the room. Energy per op can be good and the chip still loses because cost is area, and photonics is a hundred times worse per weight. Opticore's answer is to stop storing weights in silicon at all and stream them as pulses; the price is that weights now move on every use, so they inherit the GPU's arithmetic-intensity problem and an HBM stack. The mesh can play the same game at block level if the phase shifters switch in nanoseconds and the control die can push the bits, and unlike temporal mapping it keeps a stationary device to hang a gradient loop on. Sources: docs/footprint-tdm.md.

Scaling past one mesh: tiling

  • A Llama-70B MLP up-projection is \(8192\times28672\): \(14{,}336\) tiles of \(128^2\). Each tile is an SVD, \(W_{ij}=V\Sigma U^{\dagger}\): two meshes and \(N\) attenuators, optical depth \(\approx4N\) per tile
  • Conversions per MAC are unchanged: every tile still amortises \(N\) inputs and \(N\) outputs over \(N^2\) MACs. The partial-sum adds are \(O(N)\) per tile, digital, negligible
  • Error does not compound across tiles. Loss, splitter error and phase noise accumulate over \(4N\) stages inside a tile and are reset at the ADC. A monolithic large mesh would compound over its full depth; tiling caps it
  • Streaming: a tile reload is \(N^2 b\,E_{\mathrm{mod}}\approx130\,\mathrm{nJ}\) and an HBM fetch \(\approx0.4\,\mathrm{\mu J}\); amortised over \(M=10^4\) vectors that is \(\approx2.5\,\mathrm{fJ}\) per MAC. Same arithmetic-intensity rule as a GPU
  • Input light must reach every row of tiles: fan out optically (\(d_{\mathrm{out}}/N\) way split, \(18\,\mathrm{dB}\) here) or re-encode per tile row (\(E_{\mathrm{mod}}\) per element per row). Either way it is the laser shelf's problem, not the mesh's
[8:15] The question always comes: your mesh is 128 wide, my matrix is 28 thousand. The answer is tiling, and it is better than it sounds. Every tile is its own SVD, its output is digitised, so error is capped per tile rather than compounding over the whole matrix. The conversion cost per MAC is exactly what it was for one mesh. What tiling does cost is weight traffic, and that is the same rule every accelerator lives under: reuse each tile over enough vectors. At ten thousand tokens per weight per step, which is what training does, it is under three femtojoules per MAC.

  • The weight matrices see every token: \(M\) per tile per step is \(10^4\) to \(10^5\) in training, \(10^3\) to \(10^5\) in prefill, the concurrent-request count in decode
  • GQA and MQA shrink the KV cache and the K, V matrices, not the attention MACs. Windowed, sparse and linear attention do shrink them, and push the MLP share back toward \(60\%\)
  • Amdahl: a free MLP buys at most \(2.6\times\) at \(8\mathrm{k}\) context, \(1.4\times\) at \(128\mathrm{k}\)
Why attention wants a different circuit
  • \(QK^{T}\) and the \(V\) sum multiply two activations; nothing is stationary, so a mesh would be rewritten every token. A homodyne detector with both operands as pulse streams (Hamerly 2019, Opticore) has no state to rewrite
  • Prefill and training reuse each key across the sequence, so that unit is compute-bound. Decode uses each key once per token and is KV-bandwidth-bound on any hardware
  • So: mesh for the MLP and projections, temporal unit for attention, both for training and prefill. Decode stays a memory problem
[8:45] This is the slide that says where the mesh belongs. Two thirds of the weight work in a transformer is the MLP, and every token goes through it, so the weights sit still for tens of thousands of vectors. That is the mesh's case. Attention multiplies activations by activations, and there is no way to hold that in phases; you would be rewriting the mesh at terabits per second. The circuit that fits attention is the one where both operands stream into a detector. Put the two together and you cover training and prefill. Decode is a memory problem for everyone, including us.

What changed after 2022?

Precision collapsed
FP16 in 2017, FP8 with Hopper in 2022, FP4 with Blackwell in 2024. Inference at 4 bits is routine. The industry walked into the regime analog optics always needed.
Attention moved the bottleneck
Decode is KV-cache and weight-bandwidth bound. Softmax, normalization, and memory traffic are not matrix multiplies. A faster MVM engine attacks the wrong term.
Inference became the product
ChatGPT (Nov 2022), then reasoning models with test-time compute (2024). Inference now dominates deployed FLOPs and spend. Training chips are a market of a few buyers.
The ADC wall stayed put
Conversion energy per sample improves far slower than logic (Murmann survey). Every photonic accelerator still pays it on every activation, in and out.

Revisit the four conditions: fan-in large (still hard), precision low (now true), weights static (true for inference), data already analog (not in a datacenter).

[9:20] Two of the four flipped in our favor. The two that did not are both about where the data lives, which is the hint for the last slide.

Other applications: quantum and analog signal processing backup

Quantum photonics: exactness first
  • Meshes are the interferometers, switches, and multiplexers of linear-optical quantum computers (Carolan 2015; Xanadu and silicon platforms, Nature 2025)
  • No DAC or ADC in the data path: photons in, detector clicks out
  • Loss, extinction, and phase accuracy are what matter; accelerator calibration methods transfer directly
Analog signal processing and sensing
  • Mode sorting and unscrambling for free-space and multimode links (Miller 2013; Annoni 2017)
  • RF photonic filtering and beamforming, lidar wavefront control, optical switching
  • The signal is already light: conversions only at the boundary, weights updated at kHz by feedback
The transferable toolkit: parallel programming of feedforward meshes, power monitoring with two detectors instead of taps, and in situ gradient measurement. Each was built to make an accelerator trustworthy; each is worth more where the mesh is the signal path. Pai et al., JSTQE 2020; Nanophotonics 2023; Science 2023.

In situ backpropagation and inverse design backup

The adjoint method, as in photonic inverse design
  • Adjoint method: a gradient is the interference of a forward and a backward field (Hughes, Minkov, Fan 2018)
  • Forward input, backward error, then their sum: tap power at each phase shifter reads out \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\theta\)
Experimental results
  • 3-layer, 4-port silicon photonic network with thermal phase shifters and grating taps imaged by an IR camera
  • Analog update: one toggle applies the gradient, with no digital subtraction and no \(O(N^2)\) readback
  • Ring and moons trained on chip: \(90\) to \(98\%\) test accuracy, tracking digital training curves
  • Simulated \(64\times64\) MNIST: \(97\%\) ideal, \(95\%\) with tap noise and I/O error

Why width is hard: light through a 128-mesh backup

  • A random unitary is not a random set of phases: Haar-distributed meshes concentrate the MZIs near cross state toward the centre (top row)
  • Initialise uniformly and light collapses onto a few paths (bottom row); the gradient landscape has the same structure
  • Every error term in the energy equation compounds over the \(2N\) stages light crosses, so width buys efficiency and costs accuracy at the same rate
  • This is why training a mesh needs the device in the loop: the parametrisation is far from the matrix it represents
Backup. Use if asked why meshes have not scaled past 64 wide.

Inference versus training: the energy budget backup

Digital bars: datasheet peak dense throughput over board power. Envise: \(65.5\,\mathrm{TOPS}\) at \(78\,\mathrm{W}\) electrical plus \(1.6\,\mathrm{W}\) optical (Harris, Apr 2025). Formats differ; read as orders of magnitude.

A 2025 photonic system sits about \(10\times\) above GPU peak, with \(98\%\) of its power in electronics. The optics are not the term to optimize.

Inference: one forward pass
  • Tolerates \(4\) to \(8\) bits, the analog-friendly regime
  • Weights static, so phase holding amortizes
  • Cost is dominated by conversions, not photons
Training: forward, backward, update
  • Gradients want 16-bit dynamic range, analog's weak spot
  • In situ backprop: the backward pass is one more optical pass, and the update is \(O(1)\) per phase instead of \(O(N^2)\) memory traffic
  • But each step rewrites \(N^2\) phases, so weight write energy and speed set the budget
Be careful with the chart: peak datasheet numbers versus a measured research system. The point is not the ratio, it is which term dominates. In situ training removes the memory traffic term, but it does not touch the conversion term.
